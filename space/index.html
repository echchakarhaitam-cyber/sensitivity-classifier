A sensitive-text classifier that can say "I don't know"

Paste a document. For six kinds of personal data (identity, contact, financial, health, credentials, and GDPR Article 9 special categories) the model gives a calibrated probability, then answers yes, no or not sure: it only answers when its confidence reaches a threshold certified on held-out data to keep the error rate under the risk you choose. Runs in your browser; your text is not sent anywhere.

Trained on synthetic English documents: the study shows these guarantees hold on similar text but break on text from another domain. Code and results: github.com/echchakarhaitam-cyber/sensitivity-classifier.

answer: "not sure" means the confidence is below the certified threshold for the chosen error rate; "never answers" means no threshold could be certified for that category. conformal set: the labels that cannot be ruled out at level α; {no, yes} means "could be either", an empty set means neither label fits the calibration data well.
Model: word 1-2-gram TF-IDF + linear SVM per category, isotonic calibration (the model selected in the study), recomputed here with NumPy. Training data: Nemotron-PII (NVIDIA, CC BY 4.0) and Gretel synthetic PII finance (Apache-2.0). MIT licence.
